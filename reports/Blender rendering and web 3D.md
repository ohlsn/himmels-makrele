# Bake Realism Offline, Ship It Light

High-end Blender work rests on four habits: treat only base colour as colour and everything else as data, stack small imperfections (roughness variation, bevelled edges, curvature-driven wear) on every surface, light with a neutral HDRI plus a few shaped area lights while using AO as a mask rather than a multiply, and, for the web, show pre-rendered Cycles imagery by default and load a baked, compressed glTF only where interaction pays. One decision repeats at both ends of the pipeline and matters most for a merch shop: use the **Khronos PBR Neutral** tone mapper in Blender and in three.js/model-viewer, because it is designed to keep sRGB output equal to the input base colour in product photography ([Khronos](https://khronos.org/news/press/khronos-pbr-neutral-tone-mapper-released-for-true-to-life-color-rendering-of-3d-products)); no retrieved source tests it on printed textiles, so this is a reasoned recommendation, not a finding. For textures and HDRIs, the free CC0 triad of **Poly Haven, ambientCG and ShareTextures** covers nearly every need without licence risk, while Megascans is no longer free and Poliigon and Substance restrict redistribution, which matters because a .glb on your site hands visitors the textures. The web-delivery evidence is the strongest in this report (it was read from primary source code and docs): a first-load budget near **3 MB, 150,000 triangles and 2K textures** ([Khronos](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlimper/3DC-Asset-Creation/main/asset-creation-guidelines/full-version/sec99_PublishingTargets/PublishingTargets.md)), KTX2 compression that cut one reference model's GPU memory from 96 MB to 21 MB ([KTX guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/3D-Formats-Guidelines/main/KTXArtistGuide.md)), and a stable React 19 stack of r3f 9 and drei 10 on WebGL ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@react-three/fiber)). Build in this order: a pre-rendered mascot hero, then a tee/hoodie viewer whose colour swap reuses the shop's existing colour state, then a "Schwarm" hero and AR for framed prints. The researchers' web access was largely blocked, so most Blender claims rest on search summaries; numeric recipes are labelled "unverified", and every price and licence claim needs a check on the vendor's own page.

## Primary sources back the web half; search summaries back the Blender half

The evidence is lopsided, and the reader should weight it accordingly. The web-delivery findings (glTF export, three.js loaders, glTF Transform, model-viewer, Khronos budgets, npm versions) were read from primary source files and docs, for example [Blender's glTF add-on manual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO/main/docs/blender_docs/scene_gltf2.rst) and the [glTF Transform CLI source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/donmccurdy/glTF-Transform/main/packages/cli/src/cli.ts). For the Blender half, only the [AgX](https://github.com/EaryChow/AgX) and [Intel OIDN](https://github.com/RenderKit/oidn) READMEs were opened directly; everything else (texture, rendering, AO and HDRI guidance) comes from search-result summaries of manual pages, forum threads, tutorials and vendor listings. In what follows, a citation marks a sourced claim, with aggregator and vendor sources named as such. The word "unverified" marks practitioner background knowledge that no retrieved source backs, and it is collected in tables so you can test it rather than trust it. Prices and licence terms were never read on a vendor page, so each one carries a "verify" flag. The version baseline is **Blender 5.2 LTS, released 14 July 2026 with two years of support** ([Ubuntu Handbook](https://ubuntuhandbook.org/index.php/2026/07/blender-5-2-released-with-new-lts-with-2-years-of-support/)); 5.3 is in development, and the glTF add-on on the main branch already declares 5.3 as its minimum ([Blender-IO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO/main/addons/io_scene_gltf2/__init__.py)). ## Colour discipline and layered imperfection do most of the texture work

### Treat only base colour as colour

In Blender's PBR workflow only Base Color (and emission) is sRGB. Roughness, metallic, normal, height, AO and alpha are data and belong on **Non-Color**, with normal maps routed through a Normal Map node ([cgbookcase](https://www.cgbookcase.com/learn/how-to-use-pbr-textures-in-blender), [uhiyama-lab](https://uhiyama-lab.com/en/notes/blender/materials-shading-pbr-workflow/)). Blender expects **OpenGL normals** (green channel up); a DirectX map, the Unreal convention, inverts bumps and dents until its green channel is flipped ([CGAxis](https://cgaxis.com/which-normal-map-should-i-use-opengl-or-directx/), [Blender API](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.1/bpy.types.ShaderNodeNormalMap.html)). Substance Painter ships a "Blender (Principled BSDF)" export template that handles the conversion ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/substance-painter-and-blender/1507370)). For fine relief, the Bump node's Distance multiplies height in metres, so a Distance near **0.001 with Strength around 2** approximates 1-2 mm of real relief, whereas the default of 1 m makes bumps absurd ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/should-bump-node-distance-ever-really-be-higher-than-0-001/1418023)); bump maps also want Cubic interpolation ([manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/sr/4.3/render/shader_nodes/textures/image.html)). True displacement is heavier: Cycles adaptive subdivision tessellates by pixel size, but the retrieved 4.1/4.2 manuals list it under the Experimental feature set, and whether that still holds in 5.x is unconfirmed ([manual 4.1](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.1/render/cycles/object_settings/adaptive_subdiv.html)). EEVEE has supported shader displacement since 4.2, with reported edge and reflection artefacts ([4.2 EEVEE notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.2/eevee)).

The Principled BSDF is now a layered model that rewards using the right layer. Version 4.0 put Coat above emission and Sheen on top, where sheen models fibres and adds a soft, velvet-like edge reflection to cloth and can also fake dust on any material ([4.0 notes](https://wiki.blender.org/release_notes/4.0/shading/)). Thin Film (iridescence, thickness in nanometres, 200-500 nm for a soap bubble) arrived in 4.2 ([manual 4.3](https://docs.blender.org/manual/zh-hant/4.3/render/shader_nodes/shader/principled.html)) and extended to metals in 5.0 ([5.0 Cycles notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/cycles/)). The 5.2 LTS adds a **Thin Wall** mode for paper, leaves and window glass that also fixes interiors rendering too dark behind glass ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/07/blender-5-2-lts-is-here-discover-its-5-key-features/)). A mackerel's iridescent skin is a natural Thin Film candidate; the glTF exporter documents an iridescence path too, though its extension list and its materials section disagree ([Blender glTF manual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO/main/docs/blender_docs/scene_gltf2.rst)).

### Professional materials are stacks of small flaws

A flat roughness value is the classic amateur tell. One glossary page claims subtle roughness variation "does more to sell realism than almost any other single texture" ([3D glossary](https://3d.irpr.agency/glossary/roughness-map/)); that is opinion, but it matches the course material surfaced for Blender photorealism, which says no surface should be perfectly glossy ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2025/09/25/secrets-of-photorealism-in-blender/)). Fingerprints, smudges and wipe marks are colour-independent greyscale maps that feed roughness, bump or anisotropy ([Poliigon](https://www.poliigon.com/textures/surface-imperfections/smudges)), and procedural packs such as Imperfekt (65+ imperfections) advise combining several at low strength ([Imperfekt docs](https://blendermarket.com/products/imperfekt/docs)). The Bevel node rounds edges in shading only so edges catch highlights, but the manual calls it "a very expensive shader" costing about **20% render time** and suggests it for stills or baking ([manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/render/shader_nodes/input/bevel.html)). Wear follows curvature: convex edges chip and brighten, concave cavities collect grime ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/learn/tutorials/procedural-edge-wear-blender), [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/blender-guide-procedural-wear-from-a-to-z)), and dust masks use upward-facing normals ([CG Cookie](https://cgcookie.com/community/10412-procedural-edge-wear-and-cavity-dirt)). The Object Info node's Random output gives each duplicate its own colour or roughness shift ([manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/id/4.0/render/shader_nodes/input/object_info.html)). Procedurals offer unlimited resolution and low memory but cost render time, while scans win in extreme close-ups ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/products/procedural-concrete--asphalt-plaster-material)); the best-supported synthesis is a hybrid of authored or scanned base maps plus procedural roughness and hue breakup, though no single source prescribes it.

### Texel density, tiling and memory decide whether close-ups hold

Pick a texel density first and derive texture size from it. Game guides use **512-1024 px/m** for third-person and **1024-2048 px/m** for first-person cameras ([Seeles](https://www.seeles.ai/features/tools/dcc-texel-density-uv-unwrap-guide), [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/texture-resolution-guide-games-512-1k-2k-4k)); a product close-up gets nearer than those cameras ever do, so treat them as a floor. Commercial tee models ship 2K maps ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/products/t-shirt-print)) and a premium mockup scene advertises roughly 1M polygons with 6K renders ([Mavvisuals](https://mavvisuals.gumroad.com/l/BlenderStudioTshirtMockup)); both are vendor marketing, but they show the merch norm. To hide tiling, Voronoi "texture bombing" randomises each repeat with no noticeable memory or render cost ([BlenderNation Bazaar](https://bazaar.blendernation.com/?p=9752)), stochastic cell untiling needs a Vector Rotate node to keep normal maps aligned ([A23D](https://www.a23d.co/blog/how-to-make-textures-seamless-blender)), and hex tiling is available as a node ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/products/hex-mapping)). Textures are the largest memory consumer, and a retail-blog tip suggests 4K for hero textures and 2K for background ones ([Evetech](https://evezone.evetech.co.za/quick-bytes/how-to-fix-out-of-vram-errors-when-rendering-a-scene-in-blender); medium-low confidence). Blender 5.2 LTS adds a **Cycles texture cache** that converts each image into a tiled, multi-resolution .tx file and loads only the tiles a frame needs, trading disk space and a little speed for memory ([Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/05/12/blender-5-2-lts-targets-texture-heavy-renders/)). For one garment on one frame, VRAM is unlikely to bind, so print and hero textures can stay at 4K-8K (inference).

### Material recipes for fabric, wood, metal, glass and gelcoat

For fabric, Sheen supplies the fuzz and a weave normal/height map supplies structure; commercial weave systems output both ([Weave Engine](https://superhivemarket.com/products/weave-engine)). The likeliest structure for printed apparel is two layers, a fabric base and a print layer with the weave still showing through the ink, but that is synthesis, and no source describes how DTG or screen-print ink looks in render terms. For simulated drape, subdivide to level 4-6, use the cotton preset and pin the shoulders and neck ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/how-to-make-a-t-shirt-in-blender)). For wood, make grain lines rougher than the smooth areas between them ([CGAxis](https://cgaxis.com/wood-pbr-materials-in-blender/)); procedural packs combine growth rings, knots, gaps and worn edges ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/products/pro-wood)), and a Coat layer stands in for a clear lacquer where a finish is glossy (inference from the 4.0 layer model). Brushed metal is a noise texture stretched along one axis driving colour and roughness, plus anisotropy oriented by the Tangent socket ([BlenderNation Bazaar](https://bazaar.blendernation.com/?p=14849), [CG Cookie](https://cgcookie.com/community/10038-difference-between-tangent-socket-and-anisotropic-rotation)); the only source that mentions engine support says Cycles-only and is an older doc, so check EEVEE yourself. For glass, IOR 1.45 suits most glass and 1.52 crown glass, with roughness 0.01 for clear and 0.5 for frosted ([CAD Kenkyujo](https://cad-kenkyujo.com/blender-glass/)). Boats are the thinnest area: gelcoat is a pigmented, glossy layer typically 0.5-0.8 mm thick ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelcoat)) and coats generally sit at IOR 1.45-1.55 ([Tripo3D](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/hd-model-clearcoat-workflow-for-automotive-and-plastics)), but no Blender-specific gelcoat recipe was found, so the starting values in the scene-recipe table in the AO and HDRI section are unverified.

A texture printed on a garment is the product itself, which sits closest to the "standalone" territory that Substance and Poliigon prohibit, so use CC0 or self-made textures there. A .glb or three.js viewer exposes its textures to download, which reads as redistribution "in an extractable state" under Poliigon's wording (my reading of the clause, not Poliigon's stated position); confirm with Poliigon support before shipping paid textures to a public viewer, or bake and downscale. Rendered stills on the shop are fine under any paid library that you hold at a professional or business tier, and a Hobbyist tier does not cover client work, which a freelance creative director will hit. CC0 waives copyright only: logos or trademarks visible in a scan or HDRI are not cleared (general knowledge, not sourced here). Licence terms and plan structures for Megascans, Poly Haven's add-on and Poliigon have all changed since 2024, so keep an asset log with source URL, licence, date and plan tier per project.

## Cycles with OIDN and a neutral view transform delivers the finals

### Engine, sampling and denoising

Cycles remains the choice for physically accurate stills; EEVEE Next, shipped in 4.2 with screen-space ray tracing and Virtual Shadow Maps, suits fast look-development and turntables ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/07/eevee-next-finally-arrives-in-blender)). For Cycles finals, adaptive sampling renders to a **Noise Threshold** (typical values 0.1 to 0.001, lower is cleaner, 0 lets Cycles choose), and OpenImageDenoise is the default and typically highest-quality denoiser ([manual 4.1](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.1/render/cycles/render_settings/sampling.html)). Give the denoiser guiding passes; the manual recommends at least Albedo because colour alone blurs detail ([Denoise node manual](https://www.blender.org/manual/en/compositing/types/filter/denoise.html)). OIDN 2.x runs on Intel Xe, NVIDIA Turing through Blackwell, AMD RDNA 2-4 and Apple silicon, and takes HDR input, so denoise before tone mapping ([OIDN](https://github.com/RenderKit/oidn)). An aggregator guide puts final sample counts at 512-1024 for simple product shots and 2048-4096 for glass- and SSS-heavy scenes, and warns that a 0.01 threshold with a 200-sample cap will not clean the image ([CGAxis](https://cgaxis.com/complete-blender-render-setup-guide-for-photorealistic-results-2026/)). The noise levers are documented: clamp indirect light separately because bounced rays cause more fireflies, and switch off reflective and refractive caustics, "a common source of noise" ([manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/2.81/render/cycles/render_settings/light_paths.html)). The Light Tree, on by default since 3.5, works best with physically plausible lights, so use ray-visibility and custom-falloff cheats sparingly ([3.5 notes](https://wiki.blender.org/release_notes/3.5/cycles/)). **Light linking** (4.x) lets a rim or label-readability light affect only the product, and shadow linking separately decides what blocks it; linking emissive meshes is Cycles-only ([manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.3/render/lights/light_linking.html)). Cycles spills to system RAM when VRAM fills, which is slower but usually still faster than CPU ([GPU manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/render/cycles/gpu_rendering.html)).

### Colour: pick the view transform per asset

AgX is a sigmoid with **16.5 stops** of range that attenuates saturated colour smoothly towards white; its Punchy look restores some saturation, and it supports wide-gamut working spaces ([AgX](https://github.com/EaryChow/AgX)). AgX proponents say Standard clips bright colours and Filmic skews their hue ([DevTalk](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/blender-support-for-aces-academy-color-encoding-system/13972/209)); that is a contested community characterisation. Blender 5.0 added ACES 1.3 and 2.0 views and HDR display output ([Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/09/02/blender-5-0-ships-built-in-aces-2-0-view-transform/)), but no side-by-side shows whether ACES 2.0 escapes the hue-skew critiques aimed at ACES 1.x. **Khronos PBR Neutral**, added to Blender in 4.2, targets product photography where HDR values are confined to small highlights ([Blender 4.2 notes](https://wiki.blender.org/release_notes/4.2/rendering/)); whether it ships under that name in 5.x is unverified. The position that follows: use Neutral (or Standard) for any still where a print colour must match the Printful design file, and AgX with a contrast look for atmospheric, high-dynamic-range hero images such as a boat at golden hour. The steelman for AgX everywhere is its graceful highlights; the cost is desaturated brand colours. Whichever you pick, render and keep scene-linear EXR and apply the transform last; half-float EXR is the usual compromise (unverified).

### Camera, light and compositing

Blender's camera is physical: a lower F-Stop deepens the blur, Bokeh Blades sets aperture polygon count (minimum 3), and Aperture Ratio gives anamorphic bokeh ([manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/ar/4.5/render/cameras.html)). Lens distortion, chromatic aberration, vignette and grain are post-stage effects; vendors claim the native chromatic aberration also distorts the image centre ([Uber Compositor](https://blendermarket.com/products/uber-compositor), vendor marketing), so keep every one of them very subtle (unverified general practice; no source gave grain, vignette or aberration amounts). For studio light, the three-point rule is a key at roughly 45 degrees, a fill near half the key's strength and a back light for separation, and a large area light acts as a softbox ([uhiyama-lab](https://uhiyama-lab.com/en/notes/blender/lighting-fundamentals-realistic-light/)); for god rays, fill a cube with a volume at **density near 0.1** and use a low sun, where a larger sun angle softens the rays ([Evermotion](https://evermotion.org/tutorials/show/12530/creating-godrays-in-blender-tip-of-the-week)). Render a multilayer EXR so grading stays editable: Cryptomatte gives anti-aliased object and material mattes, the AO pass is a 0-1 greyscale, and Denoising Data supports a second-pass animation denoise ([passes manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/render/layers/passes.html)). Since 4.2 the compositor runs on the GPU ([4.2 notes](https://wiki.blender.org/release_notes/4.2/compositor/)), so colour-variant masks and glare are cheap to iterate; one user reported about 35-40 s dropping to 6 s ([DevTalk](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/improved-render-compositor-feedback-and-discussion/33377), anecdote). For merch batches, Python in headless Blender can set engine, samples, resolution and transparency and loop over cameras ([Super Renders Farm](https://superrendersfarm.com/article/render-multiple-cameras-blender)), and the open-source [Batch Render](https://batch-render.readthedocs.io/en/latest/usage.html) tool already renders multiple angles and turntables; a variants-times-angles loop that writes WebP into `public/assets/shop/` is the obvious fit (inference). For learning, the notes confirm Blender Guru, Gleb Alexandrov (Creative Shrimp), Ian Hubert and Blender Secrets ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/blender-channels-for-3d-artists/)) and BlenderNation's 120-channel list ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2025/08/12/blender-tutorials-huge-youtube-channels-starter-pack-120/)); no creators specific to boats or tiny-house visualisation were found.

## AO belongs in masks and bakes; the HDRI belongs in the light

### Four things called ambient occlusion

The notes' mental model, which is background knowledge and not sourced, separates four tools. The AO shader node is a per-material mask or multiplier; real global illumination in Cycles produces physically correct occlusion for free; the AO render pass is a compositing aid and adds no light; and baked AO is a static texture for engines that cannot compute it. The AO node's Distance sets how far other objects count as occluders, **Inside** flips it to detect convex edges (the basis of edge-wear masks), and Only Local is Cycles-only ([manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/dev/render/shader_nodes/input/ao.html)). EEVEE Next rebuilt AO as "horizon scan" so results track Cycles more closely, with a Thickness control: low values avoid over-occlusion from foreground geometry, too low under-occludes ([PR 108398](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/108398)). The AO pass is multiplied onto a colour image in the compositor ([manual 4.5](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.5/render/layers/passes.html)), a bug report notes that the multiply misbehaves on background pixels ([T95244](https://developer.blender.org/T95244)), and another that the AO node ignores bevelled edges ([T90134](https://developer.blender.org/T90134)). For worn edges, Pointiness gives subtle results that need a ColorRamp for contrast ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2020/10/06/blender-secrets-worn-edges/)), and Pointiness and Bevel need dense geometry and are Cycles-only ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/products/edge-wear-)).

No source lists AO mistakes, so the following is practitioner knowledge to test: multiplying a full-strength AO pass onto an already GI-lit render double-counts occlusion and looks dirty (try 20-40% strength); the default 1 m Distance darkens a 10 cm product; AO should not dim mirror-like specular; AO and other masks are Non-Color; compositing multiplies belong in linear space before the view transform; and uniform, noise-free wear masks read as procedural.

For the web, glTF stores occlusion in the **red channel** of a texture that can share an image with roughness (green) and metallic (blue) ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.1/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html)). Cycles baking needs a UV map, an image target and the scene's own render settings, so a low-sample scene bakes noisy AO, and a margin (Extend or Adjacent Faces) protects seams ([baking manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/render/cycles/baking.html)). In three.js, `aoMap` is single-channel red while `lightMap` is RGB, and baking AO still needs other light sources at runtime ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/will-light-map-that-store-as-ao-map-in-blender-cause-any-lighting-data-loss/25030)). For merch viewers, the unverified but sensible choice is a baked soft contact-shadow plane plus an environment light, because three.js AO maps affect only ambient light.

### HDRI resolution, rotation and where it shows

Poly Haven calls 16K its standard texture-map resolution going forward ([Dev Log](https://blog.polyhaven.com/dev-log-01/)), and its HDRI technical standard lists a 16K minimum published resolution ([docs](https://docs.polyhaven.com/en/technical-standards/hdris), search snippet), but resolution should follow use. A game-asset guide advises **1K-2K for anything in a browser**, 1K when the sky is never visible, and 8K-16K only for offline rendering ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/game-assets/free-hdri-skies), secondary). Setup is an Environment Texture in the World, rotated through a Mapping node's Z value and scaled by Background Strength ([A23D](https://www.a23d.co/blog/how-to-use-hdri-environment-in-blender)); a rotation sweep to place the key reflection is the cheapest quality gain, and exposure belongs in Film settings, not View Transform gamma (both unverified). Use a 32-bit or half-float .hdr/.exr, since an 8-bit "HDRI" clips the sun (unverified).

To light with an HDRI but show a different background, mix two Background nodes with Light Path > **Is Camera Ray** as the factor ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/hdri-for-lighting-but-not-background/1201155), [thread 2](https://blenderartists.org/t/render-with-hdri-lighting-but-without-hdri-image/670376)); for cut-out shop images, Film > Transparent is the simpler cross-engine route (unverified). The Shadow Catcher flag, under Object > Visibility > Mask, keeps only the shadow over a flat backdrop ([Versluis](https://www.versluis.com/2023/05/creating-a-shadow-catcher-in-blender-for-cycles-and-eevee/)); ground projection is add-on territory ([Projection Master](https://superhivemarket.com/products/projection-master--hdri-ground-projection/docs)), and no native feature was confirmed. Noise control is documented: World MIS (Auto or Manual) should stay on for HDRIs with small bright suns, and a higher importance-map resolution samples them better at some memory cost ([5.1 manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.1/render/cycles/world_settings.html)). Bundled studio HDRIs have produced fireflies from negative values around highlights, fixed by swapping the HDRI ([DevTalk](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/studiolight-hdris-cause-fireflies-in-cycles/11632)); users also report Clamp Indirect near 10 and portals as mitigations ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/impossible-to-eliminate-fireflies-cycles-2-79/1145735)). EEVEE Next converts the brightest world light into a real sun via a Threshold, Angle and Use Shadow setting ([PR 121455](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/121455)). The Nishita sky offers Sun Size, Elevation, Altitude (0 for sea level), Air, Ozone and Dust controls and is bright enough to need Film exposure turned down ([manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/ja/4.5/render/shader_nodes/textures/sky.html)); you can match its sun to an HDRI's sun ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/sky-texture-hdri-how-to-do-it-correctly/1468835)).

For studio looks, Poly Haven's studio category is the free starting point (inference: paid packs add little unless a specific look is needed), and ambientCG adds CC0 HDRIs; HDRI-Skies caps its free tier at 4K ([illustrarch](https://illustrarch.com/articles/76852-free-hdri-websites.html), aggregator; verify). Paid packs target specific looks: Greyscalegorilla's Pro Studios Metal Vol 2 (65 setups at 8K) ([Greyscalegorilla](https://greyscalegorilla.com/product/hdri-collection-pro-studios-metal-volume-2)) and Ambient Base HDRIs, marketed to replace the "empty void" ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/products/ambient-base-hdris)); their licence terms were not read. On reflections, the notes offer only practitioner knowledge: shiny objects mirror the environment, not the light, so a uniform HDRI makes flat, plastic metal, and shape comes from one large softbox, one or two strip lights, a black flag and a white bounce card, with emissive cards set invisible to the camera.

## Pre-render by default; load baked glTF only where interaction pays

### Choose realtime or pre-rendered per asset

The two proven exemplars split along this line. Apple's AirPods Pro page is a `<canvas>` fed by roughly 60-180 pre-extracted frames mapped to scroll position, with no video and no WebGL for the main effect ([SetProduct](https://www.setproduct.com/blog/how-to-3d-animate-scroll), a third-party description), and it won Awwwards Site of the Month ([Awwwards](https://awwwards.com/airpods-pro-wins-site-of-the-month-january.html)). Bruno Simon's driveable three.js portfolio won Awwwards Site of the Year 2020 with 400,000+ visits ([Pastel](https://usepastel.com/blog/how-a-design-portfolio-got-the-attention-of-400-000-visitors)); it is a brand destination, not a conversion tool. Shopify's 3D/AR numbers are vendor and aggregator claims from 2019-2022: shoppers who interacted with Rebecca Minkoff's 3D models were 44% more likely to add to cart and 27% more likely to order, and Bumbleride reported +33% conversion ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/shopify-launches-3d-model-video-integration-for-merchants/574459), [Sayduck](https://sayduck.com/3d-and-ar-statistics-2022)). Those categories are driven by shape and scale uncertainty, so expect a smaller effect for a print-on-demand tee (inference).

The decision rule, consistent with the sources: if the visitor cannot meaningfully change camera or light, pre-render; if they need orientation, colour switching or AR, go realtime. Realtime handles cloth drape, fur, volumetrics, caustics, true glass and heavy depth of field poorly (inference). Scrubbing a `<video>` by `currentTime` stutters (seek lag of 50-150 ms) and an all-intra 1080p clip can exceed 100 MB ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/scroll-scrubbed-video-stutters-fix), snippet), so use WebP frame sequences on a canvas, driven here by Framer Motion's `useScroll` since the project already ships it (inference). Transparent video needs HEVC-with-alpha for Safari plus VP9 WebM for everyone else ([Rotato](https://rotato.app/blog/transparent-videos-for-the-web), snippet). A hybrid fits the shop's data model well: render a short turntable per colour in `colors[]` and let visitors drag-scrub frames with no WebGL at all; at 36 frames of roughly 20-40 KB each, that is about 0.7-1.4 MB per colour (my arithmetic on an unsourced size estimate).

### Export and bake from Blender

Blender's glTF exporter handles core metal-rough PBR plus clearcoat, transmission, volume, sheen, specular, IOR, anisotropy, dispersion, emissive strength, texture transform, GPU instancing, Draco and Meshopt ([manual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO/main/docs/blender_docs/scene_gltf2.rst)). It reads Image Texture nodes and constant values, so bake procedural networks to images first (the manual implies this; no source tests it). Normals must pass through a Normal Map node in tangent space, and occlusion has no Principled socket: it needs a custom `glTF Material Output` node group with an image on its Occlusion input, with R=occlusion, G=roughness, B=metallic in one packed image. Transmission is flagged by the manual itself as having limited engine support. Defaults need changing for web: Apply Modifiers is off, images export as Automatic with JPEG quality 75, there is no AVIF option, and Meshopt is off ([Blender-IO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO/main/addons/io_scene_gltf2/__init__.py)). Export uncompressed from lossless sources and compress last, because KTX guidance warns that JPEG artefacts get amplified by block compression ([KTX Artist Guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/3D-Formats-Guidelines/main/KTXArtistGuide.md)) and glTF Transform's own `copy` command strips Draco to avoid a lossy round trip ([CLI source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/donmccurdy/glTF-Transform/main/packages/cli/src/cli.ts)). Bake tangent-space normals with Selected to Active (tune ray distance or cage extrusion until no rays miss), bake base colour with the Color pass only so lighting does not get baked in, and keep UVs on 4x4 pixel boundaries ([KTX Artist Guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/3D-Formats-Guidelines/main/KTXArtistGuide.md)). If the exact Cycles look must survive rotation, bake lighting into base colour and export `KHR_materials_unlit` at the cost of any relighting (inference). UDIM tiles export as separate images, so build one atlas for web.

### Compress, budget and validate

The Khronos 3D Commerce publishing targets give the clearest budgets, and the table also shows a conflict to resolve by checking the current Asset Creation Guidelines 2.0, which were not readable: an earlier blog snippet said under 5 MB and 100,000 triangles ([Khronos blog](https://www.khronos.org/blog/3d-commerce-working-group-releases-real-time-asset-creation-guidelines-to-assist-artists-create-efficient-reliable-models-for-retail-and-e-commerce)).

| Target | File size | Triangles | Draw calls (recommended, absolute) | Textures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-item mobile AR / 3D catalogue | 3 MB | 150,000 | under 20 (500) | 2K JPG |
| Single-item desktop 3D web view | 3 MB | 250,000 | under 100 (800) | 2K JPG |
| Web planning tool | 1 MB | 40,000 | under 5 (50) | 1K |
| Apple AR Quick Look | not stated | 100,000 | not stated | one 2048 set, 10 s animation, iPhone 7 floor ([Apple forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/693867)) |

The first three rows come from the Khronos [Publishing Targets](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlimper/3DC-Asset-Creation/main/asset-creation-guidelines/full-version/sec99_PublishingTargets/PublishingTargets.md). The primary optimiser is glTF Transform: `optimize` defaults to Meshopt geometry compression, 2048 px textures and lossy simplification at error 0.0001, and with `--texture-compress ktx2` it encodes normal, occlusion and metal-rough textures as UASTC and everything else as ETC1S ([CLI source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/donmccurdy/glTF-Transform/main/packages/cli/src/cli.ts)). A sensible baseline composed from those options is `gltf-transform optimize in.glb out.glb --compress meshopt --texture-compress ktx2 --texture-size 2048`, with `--simplify false` for hero assets (inference); `gltfpack -cc -tc` is the alternative ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/master/gltf/README.md)). KTX2 matters for GPU memory, not just download size: Khronos measured a lamp model falling from **96 MB to 21 MB** of GPU memory and a duck texture from 1.5 MB to 277 KB ([KTX Artist Guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/3D-Formats-Guidelines/main/KTXArtistGuide.md)). By simple arithmetic, a 2048-pixel RGBA8 texture occupies about 16 MiB uncompressed and 4 MiB block-compressed, which hurts most on iOS Safari. Meshopt decodes fast and also compresses animation and morph targets; Draco is geometry-only and needs a WASM decoder ([CLI source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/donmccurdy/glTF-Transform/main/packages/cli/src/cli.ts)), and model-viewer does not enable the Meshopt decoder by default ([docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/model-viewer/master/packages/modelviewer.dev/data/docs.json)). Self-host the decoders; model-viewer's Draco and KTX2 transcoders default to a Google CDN, and the GDPR angle for a DE/NL audience is my inference. Validate with glTF-Validator and `gltf-transform inspect` ([validator](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Validator/main/README.md)), compare in the Khronos Sample Viewer and a three.js viewer ([three-gltf-viewer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/donmccurdy/three-gltf-viewer/main/README.md)), and test on a real iPhone and a mid-range Android.

### Match the Blender look in the browser

three.js defaults to no tone mapping, exposure 1.0 and sRGB output, and offers AgX and Neutral, the latter "based on the Khronos 3D Commerce Group standard tone mapping" ([constants.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/master/src/constants.js), [WebGLRenderer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/master/src/renderers/WebGLRenderer.js)); model-viewer exposes the same choice as `tone-mapping="neutral"` ([docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/model-viewer/master/packages/modelviewer.dev/data/docs.json)). Blender's Neutral proposal states the goal of matching renderers such as three.js and Filament exactly ([Blender issue 118824](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/118824), snippet). That makes "Neutral on both sides, Look None, Exposure 0" the most direct match (inference). Matching AgX is not guaranteed; a three.js forum thread questions whether its AgX equals Blender's ([discourse](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/is-agx-tonemapping-implemented-correctly/60609), title only). Use the same HDRI as Blender's World and as `scene.environment`, tune with `environmentIntensity` rather than renderer exposure, and calibrate by rendering one sphere or ColorChecker in both (inference; no source quantifies the colour difference or maps Blender light units to three.js). For delivery, a 1K-2K equirectangular .hdr is the best-supported format; Ultra HDR JPEG is far smaller but needs `UltraHDRLoader`, and KTX2 UASTC HDR is the newest path ([UltraHDRLoader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/master/examples/jsm/loaders/UltraHDRLoader.js), [KTX2Loader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/master/examples/jsm/loaders/KTX2Loader.js)). three.js r187 rebuilds PMREM generation so reflections shift slightly, so recheck side by side after upgrading ([Migration Guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiki/mrdoob/three.js/Migration-Guide.md)).

### Integrate with Next.js 16 without hurting Core Web Vitals

The stable stack is **r3f 9 with React 19**; r3f 9 pairs with React 19 and r3f 8 does not work with it (the second claim is from a search summary) ([install docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pmndrs/react-three-fiber/master/docs/getting-started/installation.mdx), [migration guide](https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/tutorials/v9-migration-guide)). On npm, `latest` is three 0.186.1, r3f 9.8.1 and drei 10.7.9, while r3f 10 and drei 11, the WebGPU-first line, remain on the `alpha` tag ([three](https://registry.npmjs.org/three/latest), [r3f](https://registry.npmjs.org/@react-three/fiber), [drei](https://registry.npmjs.org/@react-three/drei)). WebGPU now ships in Chromium 113+ (Android from 121, Linux only for Intel Gen12+ from 144), Safari 26 and Firefox 141 on Windows, while Firefox on Linux and Android is still behind a flag or in Nightly ([gpuweb](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiki/gpuweb/gpuweb/Implementation-Status.md)); coverage claims conflict, about 76% by caniuse-derived figures versus about 95% in a secondary blog ([enterno](https://enterno.io/en/s/research-webgpu-adoption-browsers-2026), [Alesta](https://alestaweb.com/haber/webgpu-1-0-rehberi-2026-tarayicida-3d-render-ai-modellerini-gpu-ile-hizlandirma)). Stay on WebGL and treat WebGPU as a later opt-in. Two install warnings: registry peer-dependency summaries looked wrong, so run `npm view @react-three/fiber@9.8.1 peerDependencies`, and a drei issue reports "invalid hook call" from a duplicate React copy, so run `npm ls react` ([drei#2430](https://github.com/pmndrs/drei/issues/2430)).

WebGL cannot run during server rendering, so load the scene with `next/dynamic` and `ssr: false` and a sized placeholder ([Noqta](https://www.noqta.tn/en/tutorials/react-three-fiber-nextjs-3d-interactive-web-2026)); that the dynamic call must itself sit in a Client Component is unverified because nextjs.org was blocked, though `ProductDetail.tsx` is already a client component ([ProductDetail.tsx](../src/components/shop/ProductDetail.tsx)). Use `frameloop="demand"` with `invalidate()`, `PerformanceMonitor` or adaptive `dpr`, instancing for repeated objects and disposal on unmount ([r3f docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pmndrs/react-three-fiber/master/docs/advanced/scaling-performance.mdx)). For several 3D slots on one page, drei's `View` renders many DOM regions from one canvas ([View.tsx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pmndrs/drei/master/src/web/View.tsx)). drei also supplies `Environment`, `Lightformer`, `ContactShadows`, `Decal`, `Html`, `PresentationControls` and `ScrollControls` ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pmndrs/drei/master/README.md)). The vitals targets are LCP under 2.5 s and INP under 200 ms, and a static image on mobile wins on both ([Hontran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/core-web-vitals-for-animation-heavy-sites)). The shop already has the poster: the `pf_mock_*` Printful mockups in `public/assets/shop/` can serve as the LCP image and the no-WebGL and reduced-motion fallback, with the canvas fading in over a reserved aspect ratio so layout shift is zero (inference). Mount the canvas on tap or when visible, keep product text, JSON-LD and alt text in server HTML because canvas content is invisible to crawlers, and add keyboard orbit and a reduced-motion branch (all inference).

### What to build first

Ranked by the researchers' own judgement of impact against effort, not by measured data: first, a **pre-rendered mackerel hero** with a scroll-scrubbed turntable (low-medium effort, high impact, very low risk). Second, a **tee/hoodie viewer on `/shop/[productId]`** using r3f, `Decal` or a UV print layer ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/best-way-to-render-images-text-on-model-textures-decals-etc/48608)), with the existing `ProductColor` state tinting the garment and checkout untouched ([ProductDetail.tsx](../src/components/shop/ProductDetail.tsx)); it must read as illustrative because it can never contradict the Printful mockup the customer receives, and how kids and baby size families would appear is unresolved. Third, a "Schwarm" hero of instanced fish with one mackerel swimming against the flow, matching the brand line "Mit dem Schwarm schwimmen" ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)). Fourth, `<model-viewer>` AR for framed prints, since it generates USDZ for iOS on the fly for static models ([docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/model-viewer/master/packages/modelviewer.dev/data/docs.json)); AR is low value for apparel. Last, a virtual gallery, which should wait because only Wolf and Giraffant exist as finished artworks ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)). Gaussian splats are now a ratified glTF extension, but three.js loads them only through a plugin ([KHR_gaussian_splatting](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_gaussian_splatting/README.md), [GLTFLoader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/master/examples/jsm/loaders/GLTFLoader.js)); they suit captured real objects such as a finished piece of furniture and stay out of the critical path.

## Conclusion

The notes converge on two decisions that propagate through the whole pipeline: a neutral colour path (Khronos Neutral in Blender, three.js and model-viewer) and a CC0-or-self-made texture policy for anything printed or shipped to a browser. Both protect brand colours and legal exposure end to end, and both are decisions to make once, up front. The second implication is a role split. Blender's job for this shop is art direction (the mascot, lifestyle imagery, furniture, boat and tiny-house visuals), while Printful's mockups remain the product truth, so the 3D layer stays illustrative and earns its place only through colour switching and print AR. The sourced evidence favours doing the expensive work offline and keeping the browser side small.

What remains open is mostly material-specific. Nobody sourced how DTG or screen-print ink renders, numeric gelcoat or wood recipes, whether Printful offers usable garment 3D data, or any Blender-to-three.js colour-difference measurement. A one-day calibration (the same HDRI and sphere rendered in Blender with Neutral and in three.js or model-viewer) would settle the colour question, and reading the licence pages of any paid library before it touches a web-delivered or printed asset would settle the legal one. Also confirm in the 5.2 UI whether adaptive subdivision is still experimental and what the EEVEE horizon-scan panel calls its controls, and run the npm peer-dependency check before adding three.js to a project that has none yet.
